**One document, three names.** Dummy ticket, onward ticket and flight itinerary are the same reservation sold for three situations. Use whichever name matches the reader's search, and say plainly that they are the same thing where the distinction confuses people. The difference is the audience: a consulate, a check-in agent, or a border officer.

**Price is validity, not route.** The tier buys how long the reservation stays live. One way and return cost the same.

## What we do NOT sell

Never describe any of these as something we do, and never quote a price for them:

- **Visa assistance.** We do not prepare, file, submit or track visa applications. It is planned, not live. A post that offers it makes a promise we cannot keep. Write about visa requirements freely, but the service on offer is always the document, never the application.
- Flights, or any paid airline ticket.
- Airport transfers, except as a thing we can arrange on request. Never a linked product.

## Policies worth citing in a post

- **Delivery:** 10 to 15 minutes after payment, 24 hours a day.
- **Verification:** the PNR can be checked in Amadeus, Sabre or Travelport. Some airlines show it under Manage Booking, but many never display an unpaid hold, so the GDS lookup is the reliable one. Say this honestly rather than promising every airline site will show it.
- **Rescheduled appointments:** we reissue with new dates free of charge.
- **Refunds:** full refund in one case only, where a refusal letter says the reservation had expired or could not be verified. Not for a change of plans, not for a refusal on other grounds.
- **Payment:** card through Stripe Checkout, or a payment link on request.
- **Support:** info@thedummyticket.ae, replies in 10 to 15 minutes, around the clock.

## The legal framing

This matters, because the topic attracts nervous readers:

- A reservation is a genuine booking, not a forged document. That is the whole distinction, and it should be made plainly whenever the reader might be worried.
- Consulates ask for evidence of **travel arrangements**, not a purchased fare. Article 14 of the EU Visa Code is the citable source for Schengen.
- Never claim a document guarantees a visa, an approval, or entry. Refusals turn on funds, ties and consistency.
- Never suggest presenting a reservation as a paid ticket. It is proof of a plan, and where an airline demands a **paid** onward ticket at the gate, say so rather than implying otherwise.

## Content Guidelines

- Write for someone with an appointment booked and a document missing. They are anxious and short of time.
- Be concrete: name the visa centre, the requirement, the document, the timeframe.
- Nationality changes the answer more often than people expect. A UAE resident on an Indian or Pakistani passport faces different rules from an Emirati. Say so where it matters instead of writing as if every reader is the same.
- Never invent a price, a processing time, or a rule. If it is not in this context or an official source, describe it in general terms.
- Where a rule is in flux, say that it is. Half-implemented border systems are more useful described honestly than reported as settled.

## Writing Style

Conversational and practical. Verdict first, then the detail. No em dashes. No AI filler. Short paragraphs. British English spelling.
