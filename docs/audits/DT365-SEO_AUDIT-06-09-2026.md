## What was checked

Full live crawl of all 42 indexable URLs (rendered HTML, response headers, canonical, schema, headings, link graph), the complete blog corpus pulled from the live backend API (21 published posts), robots/sitemap generation, redirect and 404 behaviour, mobile viewport rendering at 412x915 with 4x CPU and Fast-3G-class throttling, JSON-LD parse validity on every page, and the attached Google Search Console export (Chart, Countries, Devices, Pages, Queries, Search appearance; Web search, last 3 months).

## What was NOT checked

- **Production Core Web Vitals.** Numbers below come from the `next dev` Turbopack server, which ships unbundled, unminified JS. Field/lab LCP and TBT on the deployed Fly.io app will be materially better and are **not verified**. The structural performance findings (image dimensions, font strategy, render-blocking count, layout stability) are build-independent and do hold.
- **Live production response headers** on `www.dummyticket365.com`. Header checks ran against the local Next server, so any Fly.io or CDN-injected header is unverified.
- **`next build` was not run** (this audit is read-only and a build would clobber the dev server's cache), so build-time errors and the production bundle size are unverified.
- **i18n / hreflang: N/A.** There is no locale routing, no `alternates.languages`, and no market variants anywhere in the app. GSC reports 193 clicks from "Translated results", which is Google's own auto-translation, not a site feature.
- **Rating/review schema** was not assessed for policy risk because none is emitted.

# Executive summary

| Bucket | Score | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Technical SEO | 72 / 100 | 25% |
| On-page SEO | 74 / 100 | 20% |
| Content & Blog | 68 / 100 | 20% |
| AEO / GEO | 66 / 100 | 20% |
| Internal Linking | 52 / 100 | 15% |
| **Overall (weighted)** | **67 / 100** | |

The technical foundation is genuinely good: every page returns 200 with a correct self-referencing canonical, exactly one H1, valid OG and Twitter tags, and a rich JSON-LD graph that parses cleanly. The sitemap is complete and auto-generated, 404s return a real 404 with `noindex`, and trailing-slash and apex-to-www are both handled with 308/301 redirects. Product snippets are already earning 997 clicks in GSC, so the schema work is paying.

What is holding the site back is not configuration, it is three things. First, **35 internal links in blog content point at URLs that 404**, including every inbound link to the two best-performing blog posts. Second, **ten pages advertise Hotel Reservations and Travel Insurance as on-site products that cannot be bought on the site**, which contradicts the house facts, breaks the funnel and reads as unreliable to both users and language models. Third, **the QuickAnswer component is commented out on all ten landing pages**, so the site's best AEO asset exists in the codebase but ships to nobody.

GSC confirms the shape of the problem. Impressions grew from 1,514/day on 05-Jun to 7,819/day on 04-Sep (+416%) while clicks only went 29 to 69 (+138%), so sitewide CTR fell from 1.92% to 0.88%. Almost all of that new, non-converting impression volume lands on one blog post about PNRs.

## Top 10 fixes

1. Repair the 35 broken internal links in blog content (8 distinct dead targets, incl. 10 links to the top blog post).
2. Reframe the Hotel Reservations and Travel Insurance cards on all 10 landing pages as email-request services, or remove them.
3. Re-enable `QuickAnswer` on all 10 landing pages (already written, already imported, just commented out).
4. Add a real quick-answer block and FAQ to `/flight-itinerary` (311 words, avg position 26.7, the worst page on the site).
5. Rewrite title and meta for `/blog/what-is-a-pnr-...` (93,026 impressions, 0.20% CTR, position 6.9).
6. Give `/onward-ticket` blog support and a title/meta rewrite (11,611 impressions, 0.97% CTR, position 10.8, zero blog links in).
7. Fix the internal contradiction in the airline-verification post's FAQ #5 versus its own body table (house fact 2).
8. Point blog links at the 8 money pages that currently receive none (Japan, Australia, Canada, Onward, Itinerary, Lufthansa, Turkish, Air France).
9. Add `sameAs`, `telephone` and a real author entity to the Organization and BlogPosting schema; fix `areaServed: "AE"` on a global service.
10. Trim the 4 over-length meta descriptions and 4 over-length titles, and give `/faq`, `/blog` and the tag pages keyword-bearing H1s.

## Crawl hygiene

**This is the worst area of the audit.**

Crawling all 42 pages found **35 internal links resolving to 8 distinct URLs that return HTTP 404.** All of them come from blog post bodies stored in MongoDB. The cause is two authoring errors baked into the CMS content:

1. **Wrong relative depth.** Links written as `../slug` from a `/blog/slug` page resolve to `/slug` at the site root, not `/blog/slug`. Links written as `../../blog/slug` resolve correctly. Both patterns are present in the same corpus.
2. **Stale slugs.** Several links reference post slugs that were renamed and never updated.

Quality issues in otherwise-valid schema:

- **`Service.areaServed: "AE"`** on every landing page. The service is global. GSC's top markets are India (541 clicks), United States (461), Canada (271), Australia (260) and the UK (251). UAE is sixth. This actively mis-scopes the entity.
- **`Product.name` and `Service.name` are the full SEO title**, e.g. `"Dummy Ticket for Schengen Visa From $13 | Verifiable PNR"`, pipe and price included. Product snippets already earn 997 clicks at 4.07% CTR, so this is working despite itself, but the name should be the product, not the title tag.
- **Organization has no `sameAs` and no `telephone`.** A WhatsApp number exists in `src/config/contact.js` and is rendered on every page, but it is absent from schema. No social profiles are declared at all. This is the single largest entity-recognition gap.
- **`BlogPosting.author` is a bare name string**, no `@type: Person`, no `url`, no `sameAs`, no author page. Weak E-E-A-T signal.
- `/terms-and-conditions` and `/privacy-policy` emit only BreadcrumbList, missing the Organization/WebSite/WebPage graph every other page gets.
- No `datePublished` is displayed on-page (only "Updated"), and there is no `<time datetime>` element anywhere. Schema carries both dates correctly, so this is a human-trust nit rather than a machine one.

## Top 10 highest-leverage fixes

1. **Repair the 35 broken internal links** (#1). Low effort, high impact, and it unblocks the entire internal-linking bucket. The site's top blog post currently has zero working editorial inbound links.
2. **Reframe hotel and insurance as email-request** (#2). Ten pages currently promise a purchase path that 404s. Credibility error plus a dead funnel, and the correct copy is already sitting unused in the repo.
3. **Uncomment `QuickAnswer` on all 10 landing pages** (#3). Twenty lines to give every money page a verdict-first, snippet-shaped answer. The highest ratio of AI-presence gain to effort in this audit.
4. **Fix the verification post's FAQ #5 contradiction** (#7). One paragraph. It is the FAQPage-marked text most likely to be lifted by an answer engine, and it currently contradicts the post's own table.
5. **Rewrite title and meta for the PNR post** (#5). 93,026 impressions at 0.20% CTR, with 44,817 of them at position 5 returning zero clicks. Largest single CTR pool on the site.
6. **Rescue `/onward-ticket`** (#6). 11,611 impressions, position 10.8, 0.97% CTR, and zero blog links despite two posts written on exactly its intent.
7. **Rebuild `/flight-itinerary`** (#4). 311 words at position 26.7 while its own blog post ranks at 9.96 for the same intent. The clearest cannibalisation loss on the site.
8. **Point blog links at the 8 unfed money pages** (#8). Japan and Canada already convert at 5.3-5.5% CTR on tiny impression volume; they need inputs, not optimisation.
9. **Add `sameAs`, `telephone` and a real author entity** (#9). Cheap schema work that closes the biggest entity-recognition gap for both Google and LLMs.
10. **Fix `areaServed: "AE"` and trim the over-length titles and descriptions** (#10, #11). Ten minutes of edits across 13 pages, on a site where Product snippets already convert at 4.07%.
